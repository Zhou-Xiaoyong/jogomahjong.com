How to Play Mahjong Memory: 36 Tiles, 18 Pairs, No Hints

Thirty-six tiles face down, eighteen pairs, and a scoreboard with two numbers on it. Mahjong Memory does not draw new faces between games. The same eighteen come back every time; only their positions move.

Thirty-six tiles down, eighteen moves at best

The board deals 36 face-down tiles and the pair counter reads 0/18. No hint button. No shuffle. No deadline. Your score is a move count and a clock in the corner.

Eighteen is the floor: one move per pair, zero mistakes, empty board. The only control on the page is New Game, which redeals all 36 tiles and resets both counters. Players arriving from solitaire expecting a safety net will not find one here.

The same eighteen faces, every single game

This is the rule that rewrites your approach. The deck does not draw random faces. It walks a fixed list of 18 tiles, identical from one game to the next, and shuffles only their positions.

  • Winds: East, South, West, North. Four pairs.
  • Dragons: Red, Green, White. Three pairs.
  • Characters: 1, 5 and 9. Three pairs.
  • Bamboos: 1, 5 and 9. Three pairs.
  • Circles: 1, 5 and 9. Three pairs.
  • Flowers: Plum and Chrysanthemum. Two pairs.

Seven of those 18 faces are honours, which puts 39% of the pairs in winds and dragons. A full 136-tile set carries 28 honour tiles, about 21%. The memory board is far more top-heavy than a real table, and that helps: an honour tile reads at a glance, with no pips to count.

Two absences go unnoticed in a first game. No season tile appears at all, and only two of the four flowers make the list. Hunting for the Orchid is the kind of mistake that burns a whole sweep. If the names still blur together, the complete guide to how to play mahjong settles them first.

Two copies per face, never four

A traditional 136-tile set gives every face four copies. This deck gives it two, and that changes how you read the board.

Spot a Red Dragon and exactly one other Red Dragon is out there. There is no third copy hiding in a corner, and no spare to keep in reserve. That pair is the only pair.

The odds on your first blind attempt shrink accordingly. You turn one tile; the chance the next one is its partner is 1 in 35, or 2.9%. Guesswork gets punished. Sweeping gets rewarded.

The suit color does half the remembering

Positions take repetition. Colors take a minute. Each suit prints in a fixed tone:

  • Winds: dark blue.
  • Dragons and characters: red.
  • Bamboos: green.
  • Circles: black.
  • Flowers: pink.

The Portuguese name of the tile sits under the symbol in small type. That kills the 5 bamboo versus 5 circle mix-up mid-game, and it teaches the vocabulary without a lesson.

A miss costs a move and a second of waiting

Two flipped tiles make one attempt. A correct pair stays face up for the rest of the game, after a 0.3 second pause before the board redraws. A wrong pair turns back over after 1 second, and the move counter climbs by one.

The board is locked for that whole second. Clicking a third tile does nothing and costs nothing. It is the only free click in the game, and the most pointless one: jumping the gun loses the gesture and teaches you nothing.

The clock starts when the page loads

The timer runs from page load, not from your first click. Read the intro at your leisure and you begin a game already sitting at 04:00.

It counts up and eliminates nobody. Finishing in 04:00 and finishing in 09:00 leave the same empty board. The number is there to compare your own games, not to rank anyone.

Read first, match second

Because no tile blocks another, this is pure recall. No position is illegal, but some moves spend a turn without buying information.

  • Sweep in a fixed order. Left to right, row by row, the same way every time. A fixed path lowers the cost of remembering where things live.
  • Take the pair as soon as you find it. A matched pair stays visible, so postponing it saves nothing and holds a slot in your head.
  • Count reveals, not mistakes. For the first ten moves, the goal is seeing new faces. After that the board holds enough information to harvest.
  • Group by color. Four winds and three dragons are seven tiles you read instantly, with nothing to count.

A solid game lands between 24 and 30 moves. Under 24 means near-total recall. Past 35, the sweep turned into a stroll.

When memory is the wrong mode

If the appeal is stacking and blocked tiles, the Mahjong Solitaire here runs 112 tiles over four levels and makes you press Shuffle when it locks. If you want the calm without the memorizing, Relaxing Mahjong deals 108 tiles across three levels and reshuffles on its own. Memory is the only one of the three where the obstacle is you.

Your first game, four calls

Open Mahjong Memory, sweep the first row end to end without matching anything, take every pair whose location you already know, and treat 24 as the passing grade. Do not chase a time record on game one: the clock only means something once the eighteen faces look familiar.

The exception is a deal that scatters the seven honour pairs along the edges. Take winds and dragons first there, since you recognize them fastest, and leave the 5s and 9s of all three suits for the closing sweep.
