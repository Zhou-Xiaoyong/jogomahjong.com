Flowers and Seasons in Mahjong: The Eight Tiles You Never Play

Draw a flower and nobody at the table waits for you to think. You turn it face up, pull a replacement from the back of the wall, and play on. Those four flowers and four seasons are the only tiles in the set that cannot go into any meld.

Eight tiles that never reach your hand

A full set holds 144 tiles: 108 suited, 16 winds, 12 dragons, 8 bonus. The bonus eight are four flowers and four seasons, one copy each. They do not come in pairs, they cannot pung, and they cannot chow. Every other tile has an identical twin somewhere in the set. These do not.

That flips the usual decision. You never weigh whether to hold a flower or throw it. It arrives, it leaves.

Plum, orchid, bamboo, chrysanthemum

The flowers are 梅 (plum), 蘭 (orchid), 竹 (bamboo) and 菊 (chrysanthemum). Chinese painting gives this quartet its own name, the Four Gentlemen, and each plant carries a season and a character trait. Plum blossoms at the end of winter, when nothing else will. Orchid grows in a hidden valley and smells good with no audience. Bamboo bends in wind without snapping. Chrysanthemum opens in autumn, after the rest has wilted.

One reading trap sits here. The bamboo flower uses the same character as the bamboo suit (索), and the artwork looks close enough to fool a tired eye. This site labels it "Bambu-flor" for exactly that reason, so it does not get confused with the 1 Bamboo, which by the way shows no bamboo at all. It shows a sparrow.

Four characters cover the seasons

春 (spring), 夏 (summer), 秋 (autumn), 冬 (winter). One tile each, no large numeral on the face. The value from 1 to 4 prints small in the corner.

The two families are the same calendar written twice. Flowers name the season through a plant, season tiles name it outright. The two calendars do not line up. Plum is the winter flower in painting, yet season one is spring. Nothing went wrong at the factory. Flowers follow the plants, seasons follow the civil year, and no workshop ever bothered to reconcile them.

The corner number points at a seat

At a four-player table the 1 through 4 maps to a seat wind: 1 East, 2 South, 3 West, 4 North. Sit West and the flower numbered 3 and the season numbered 3 belong to you. The other six belong to somebody else, which is why nobody at the table cares about them.

Under Hong Kong scoring your own flower is worth 1 fan and your own season another 1 fan. Draw both in the same turn and you have 2 points before the hand has a shape. Under Chinese Official rules (MCR) a flower scores nothing on its own. It just gets you another draw.

What the table does when one turns up

You draw, lay it face up in front of you, take a replacement from the back of the wall, and continue without discarding. If the replacement is another flower, you repeat. The turn keeps moving either way.

The price is not tempo, it is information. Every flower laid open tells the table that tile is gone for good, and a player collecting flowers for their own seat is announcing the plan out loud. Some house rules pay a bonus for all four flowers of a group. That number changes from table to table and gets settled before the first deal, never in the middle of it.

Why riichi sets have no flowers

A Japanese set runs 136 tiles, 34 types in four copies. The bonus eight are simply absent. Modern riichi replaced them with the akadora, the red five in each suit, which is worth extra points and forces a choice the flower never forces: keep it or let it go.

American mahjong went the other way. The standard set keeps all eight flowers and adds eight jokers, for 152 tiles, and the annual league card calls for specific flowers in a good number of hands. Same tile, three weights: seat bonus in Hong Kong, a hand requirement in the US, close to nothing under Chinese Official scoring.

Where they turn up on this site

The Mahjong Solitaire board deals 112 tiles drawn from 34 types, so no flowers appear. Without an identical twin they would break the pairing rule the board runs on. The eight only show up in four-player games with real tiles on the table, where our tile data names them Plum, Orchid, Bamboo Flower, Chrysanthemum, Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter.

If a board does use the full set, the traditional solitaire rule lets any flower match any flower and any season match any season. It is the one exception to identical-pair matching, and it exists because those tiles never had a twin to begin with.

Telling the eight apart at a glance

  • Flower: plant artwork filling the face, small 1 to 4 number in the corner.
  • Season: a large character in the center where the artwork would sit.
  • Color: flowers usually run red, green and purple; seasons vary widely between sets.
  • None of the eight has a duplicate. If two tiles look alike, one is the bamboo flower and the other belongs to the bamboo suit.

Are the eight worth memorizing?

For screen play the short version is enough: bonus tiles, no melds, never part of a hand. Learn the season order and the four plants and you can read any set on the market.

The exception is a Hong Kong table, where the corner number is money. Holding flower 3 in the North seat pays zero; the same tile in the West seat pays 1 fan. Check where you are sitting before the shuffle, not after.
