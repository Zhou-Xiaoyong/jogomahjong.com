How to Play Mahjong for Beginners: A Step-by-Step Guide

If you have never touched a Mahjong tile, this guide is for you. In a few minutes you will understand the goal of the game, learn the single most important rule and be ready to clear your very first board.

First things first: there are two "Mahjongs"

When someone says "let's play Mahjong", it can mean two quite different things. Sorting this out is the first step to not getting lost:

  • Classic Mahjong: the Chinese four-player table game with turns, discards and scoring — structurally closer to a card game like rummy.
  • Mahjong Solitaire: the one-player puzzle where you clear matching pairs from a stacked board. It is by far the most played version online, and it is the one you will find on this site.

This guide focuses on Solitaire, because that is where every beginner should start. The tiles are exactly the same as in the traditional game, but you learn at your own pace — no rulebook, no scoring table and no need for three other people.

The Mahjong tiles in 2 minutes

A full set has 144 tiles. That sounds like a lot, but they fall into just a handful of groups and you will recognise all of them after a game or two.

The three numbered suits

  • Circles (筒): discs representing old Chinese coins, numbered 1 to 9.
  • Bamboos (索): green sticks, also 1 to 9. The "1 of bamboo" is usually drawn as a small bird — don't let it confuse you.
  • Characters (萬): tiles with Chinese ideograms, again numbered 1 to 9.

The honour tiles

  • Winds: East, South, West and North.
  • Dragons: red (中), green (發) and white.

Flowers and seasons

These eight special tiles come with a privilege: in Solitaire, any flower matches any other flower and any season matches any other season. They do not have to be identical.

The goal of the game

It really is this simple: empty the board by removing tiles two at a time. Click two identical tiles and they disappear. Repeat until nothing is left — that is a win.

The rule that decides everything: you can only remove a tile that is free. A tile is free when (1) no other tile sits on top of it and (2) either its left or its right side is unblocked. Tiles trapped in the middle of a row stay locked until a neighbour is cleared.

How to play Mahjong step by step

  • Step 1 — Scan the whole board before clicking. Ten seconds of reading beat ten rushed clicks.
  • Step 2 — Spot the free tiles. Start at the edges and at the top of the stacks: that is where the available moves almost always are.
  • Step 3 — Look for a pair. Two tiles with the same design, both of them free.
  • Step 4 — Click one, then the other. If the match is valid, both vanish and the tiles underneath are released.
  • Step 5 — Reassess. Every removal reshapes the board and opens new paths. Go back to step 2.
  • Step 6 — Repeat until the board is empty. No tiles left means you won.

The fastest way to internalise this is to practise: open a round of free Mahjong Solitaire in another tab and follow the steps above with a real board in front of you. Ten minutes in, the logic becomes automatic.

5 golden tips for beginners

  • Attack the tall stacks first. Stacked tiles lock everything beneath them. Dismantling the towers early opens up the board.
  • Watch out for long rows. Only the two ends are free, so clearing from the extremities is what unlocks the middle.
  • When all four copies of a tile are visible, remove the two most "trapped" ones. That way you keep the easy pair in reserve.
  • Don't spend a pair just because you see it. If both tiles are loose and easy to reach, they can wait — use your move to unlock another area instead.
  • Use hints sparingly. They save you in a pinch, but leaning on them stops you from training your own eye.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Clicking too fast. Mahjong Solitaire is not a reflex game — it is a board-reading game.
  • Forgetting flowers and seasons. Plenty of players get stuck hunting for an exact match when any flower would have worked.
  • Mixing up similar tiles. The 6, 8 and 9 of circles look alike at a glance. Count the dots calmly.
  • Giving up at the first dead end. Running out of moves is normal early on; use the shuffle and carry on.

What about classic four-player Mahjong?

Once the tiles feel familiar, the original game is well worth exploring: each player holds 13 tiles and tries to build four sets (triplets or runs) plus one pair, drawing and discarding each turn. The tiles are exactly the ones you have just learned — only the dynamic changes. If you want to know where all of this came from, take a look at the history of Mahjong and the origin of Mahjong in China: understanding what each symbol means actually helps you memorise the tiles faster.

Your next steps

Here is everything you need to take away from this guide: identical tiles form pairs, only free tiles can be removed, and the board must end up empty. That is genuinely all there is to the core rules. The rest is practice.
