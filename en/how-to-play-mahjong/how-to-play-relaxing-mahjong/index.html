How to Play Relaxing Mahjong: 108 Tiles, 54 Pairs and No Way to Lose

Three levels, 108 tiles, 54 pairs, and a clock that only counts up. Relaxing Mahjong has no losing screen. When the board locks, it reshuffles itself.

108 tiles, three levels, no spike in the middle

The board deals 108 tiles with the pair counter sitting at 0/54. That is three levels deep, one less than the solitaire here, and the shape is deliberately flat. Nobody stacked a tower in the center.

  • Base: 80 tiles. One row of 10 on top, five rows of 12 through the middle, one row of 10 along the bottom.
  • Second level: 24 tiles in 8 columns by 3 rows.
  • Top: 4 tiles in a single row, dead center.

Look at what that gives you. Fifty-six of the 80 base tiles start with nothing above them, and the two rows of 10 at the edges carry no upper layer at all. Only the 12-column band has to be uncovered.

If winds, dragons and suits still slow your reading down, the complete guide to how to play mahjong settles the names first.

Twenty faces in four copies, fourteen in two

The core set holds 34 faces. The board asks for 54 pairs, so the deal is uneven.

  • Four copies, two pairs each: four winds, three dragons, all nine characters and bamboos 1 to 4. Twenty faces, 40 pairs, 80 tiles.
  • Two copies, one pair each: bamboos 5 through 9 and all nine circles. Fourteen faces, 14 pairs, 28 tiles.

That matters at the table. With four copies you can close a pair using the two free tiles and leave the buried ones standing. With two copies the pair is fixed: either both tiles come out, or the face stays on the board in the way.

The 28 single-pair tiles are a quarter of everything you can see. Spot two 7 circles and you have found the only pair of that face in the game.

What makes a tile playable

  • Nothing on top: no tile from a higher level may share its column and row.
  • One open side: the immediate neighbor left or right, same level, has to be gone. Either side will do.

Blocking runs on the grid, not on the picture. The 8 px offset between levels hints at partial overlap, yet one second-level tile covers exactly one cell below it. A blocked tile is a disabled button, and it swallows the click without an error message.

A miss costs a move, not a tile

Two clicks make a pair. When the tiles do not match they shake for a moment, the move counter goes up by one, and nothing leaves the board. The tile from the second click stays selected, so your next click is a real attempt rather than a wasted one.

That is why moves are the only score here. Finishing in 54 and finishing in 90 both clear the same board. Fifty-four is the floor: one move per pair, zero mistakes.

Hints and shuffles are free

  • Free hint: highlights an available pair with no usage cap. The hints-used counter rises on every press, though it exists for comparison, not punishment.
  • Shuffle: re-deals the faces of the tiles still standing and leaves each one in place. Open cells stay open.
  • Automatic shuffle: after a pair leaves the board, a position with no playable pair reshuffles itself 1.2 seconds later.

Ask for a hint when no pair exists and the game says so, then reshuffles in 0.8 seconds. Even the button that finds nothing solves the problem.

Why there is no losing screen

Every face enters the deck an even number of times, two copies or four. No tile can be stranded without a partner, and a reshuffle only moves faces around, never positions.

Put those together and the board always clears. What changes is the move count and the clock. Which changes the question you ask while playing. Not "can I finish?" but "can I finish without spinning my wheels?"

A clearing order that saves moves

  • Top first. The four tiles on level 2 open the middle of level 1 and need no reading at all.
  • One row at a time, ends toward the middle. On a 12-tile row, pulling the end tile opens its neighbor, and the whole row becomes playable without touching anything else.
  • Favor the pair that uncovers something. Between two playable pairs, the higher one is worth more than the edge one, since the edge was already open.
  • Save the two rows of 10. They read fastest and serve as a reserve when the middle locks up.

The reverse route also works, and it is the usual mistake for players arriving from solitaire: clear the easy edges, bank 20 pleasant moves, then find that only the 12-column band is left with nothing obvious to take.

When relaxing is the wrong pick

Want pressure? Timed Mahjong opens at 05:00 with 112 tiles and charges a second for every miss. The solitaire version runs 112 tiles over four levels and, when it locks, waits for you to press Shuffle instead of fixing itself. Still learning to tell a bamboo from a circle? This is the right table to practice on.

Your first board, five rules

Open Relaxing Mahjong, take the four top tiles first, finish one row at a time from the ends inward, and leave the two rows of 10 for last. Keep your hands off Shuffle early on: the automatic one covers the real case, and the manual button only repeats it.

The exception is a board that buries every circle pair under the 12-column middle. Spend two or three hints in a row there, because they cost nothing and show what your eye missed.
