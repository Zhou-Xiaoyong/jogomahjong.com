How to Make a Kong in Mahjong: Four of a Kind and the Extra Draw

Four identical tiles on the table and one draw that comes off the back of the wall. A kong is not a bigger pung: it is the only meld that makes your hand physically larger.

One tile more in the hand

Three 7 of bamboo in your hand and the fourth arrives on the draw. You lay the set down, pull a tile off the back of the wall, and the tiles in front of you plus the ones in hand now add up to fourteen. At the win they will read fifteen.

Start with the arithmetic. Four sets and a pair make fourteen tiles; every kong adds one physical tile, because it takes four slots and still counts as one set.

With two kongs, the table sees sixteen tiles in front of you when you declare. No other meld touches the size of your hand.

Assembly is lopsided too. A pung of 5 of circles exists in four versions, since the choice is which three copies to use. A kong exists in one, and it swallows the whole supply of that design.

The whole stock of a design in one move

A 136-tile set carries 34 designs at four copies each. Declare a kong and all four copies sit in front of you. Nobody pungs that design again, nobody waits for a fifth copy that was never made.

The damage reaches past pungs. A kong of 5 of circles erases three chows: 3-4-5, 4-5-6 and 5-6-7. A kong of 1 of bamboo erases one, because the 1 only fits 1-2-3. Kong a middle tile and you announce that three roads in that suit are closed.

There are 34 possible kongs and not one has a backup. That changes how you read a discard: the fourth copy in your hand is worth more there than on the table, because it stays a tile nobody can count. The copy arithmetic is the same one behind the set of three, in how to make a pung.

Three ways to declare

  • Concealed. All four tiles leave your hand, two face up in the middle and two turned down at the ends. The design is visible, so the table gets the same warning as an open kong.
  • Open. You hold three and claim the discard from any seat, as with a pung. All four stay face up and the claimed tile lies sideways.
  • Added. You already have an open pung and the fourth copy arrives. The set grows in place, and this is the only declaration in the game an opponent can interrupt.

The practical split sits in the closed hand. A concealed kong opens nothing: you can still declare riichi after it and collect menzen tsumo. Open or added, the hand is open and those requirements fall with it.

The draw that comes from the back

Every kong pays for a replacement draw, and it does not come off the front of the wall. Under riichi it comes from the dead wall, the row of fourteen tiles set aside at the corner. In Hong Kong it comes off the tail.

Each kong also flips a fresh dora indicator under riichi. Declare two in one hand and you have turned two extra value tiles, for yourself and for the other three.

The wall sends the bill: every kong shortens the game by one draw. With three kongs out, everyone has three fewer draws, and hands often die in the wall.

Winning on the replacement tile has its own yaku, rinshan kaihou, worth 1 han. That is the only han a kong produces under riichi, and it comes from the draw rather than the set.

A kong erases other players' waits

Kong the 5 of circles and you have cut the 3-4, the 4-6 and the 6-7 in that suit. You also cut the pair: the two copies someone was holding for the eye of their hand no longer exist.

That makes it the most talkative meld on the table: it shows what you hold and deletes what the others could have held.

So count before you lay it down. Four of a kind in hand already blocks that design for everyone else, with nothing declared.

Four kongs, two opposite endings

Four kongs in one hand is a yakuman: suukantsu, sixteen tiles on the table and a pair closing the count.

Four kongs declared by different players end the hand with no winner. The wall cannot carry four replacements, and the round is thrown out.

Same number, opposite outcomes. Chasing the yakuman means declaring all four; killing the round means declaring the fourth and taking everyone down with it.

What each rule set pays

Under riichi a kong produces no han. It produces fu, double what the matching pung pays, plus the shot at rinshan. Rounding up in tens swallows that edge at payout.

Hong Kong counts 1 fan and lets a rival rob the added tile. Sichuan pays on the spot rather than at the end of the hand, which turns the meld into immediate income. The American league has no kong call at all: four identical tiles only count when that year's card asks for four identical tiles, and there is no replacement draw.

When it is worth declaring

Declare early when the fourth copy shows up in the first few rounds and the design is an honor: winds and dragons already pay on their own, and the replacement moves the hand forward.

Hold it when a rival is one tile from done and you cannot tell what they wait on. Losing the hand to a chankan costs more than the fu you gain.

The exception is a closed hand chasing riichi, pinfu or iipeikou. There only the concealed kong goes in, because it keeps the hand closed; open or added, the meld shuts all three doors at once.

On screen the tile leaves in pairs, and four identical tiles become two pairs: Solitaire opens 112 tiles as 56 pairs, with 22 of the 34 designs showing up four times on the board. A real kong needs four seats, discards and calls. If the set of three is still the fuzzy part, go to how to make a sequence, and the how-to-play index shows where the call fits into a round.
