How to Play Timed Mahjong: The Clock, the Bonuses and 112 Tiles

Timed Mahjong loads at 05:00 with 112 tiles on the table. That is 56 pairs at 7.3 seconds apiece, and a wrong click costs clock instead of just a move.

Five minutes, 112 tiles, 56 pairs

The board opens at 05:00 with 112 tiles to clear. A correct pair returns 2 seconds. A wrong pair takes 1 away. Run the numbers: 300 seconds up front plus 112 seconds of bonus buys 412 seconds of play, which works out to 7.3 seconds per pair. Spend eight seconds on a move and you lose by a hair.

Every time, too.

The clock starts when the page loads, not on your first click. Leaving the tab open while you read something else is the cheapest way to begin a round at 04:12.

If the winds, dragons and suits still slow you down, the complete guide to how to play mahjong settles the vocabulary before any mode with a deadline.

Four stacked layers, not a pyramid

The base runs wide through the middle and narrows at both ends: 12 columns across the central band, 4 at the top and bottom edges. Nothing above the base goes past 8 columns.

  • Base (level 0): 72 tiles in rows of 4, 8, 12, 12, 12, 12, 8 and 4.
  • Level 1: 28 tiles in rows of 6, 8, 8 and 6.
  • Level 2: 8 tiles, two rows of 4.
  • Top (level 3): 4 tiles, two rows of 2.

No flowers, no seasons. The deck draws on the core set alone, so no wildcard is coming to rescue a stranded pair late in the round.

What makes a tile playable

Two tests at once, and a blocked tile simply ignores the click.

  • Nothing on top: no tile on a higher level may share its column and row.
  • One open side: the immediate neighbour to the left or right, on the same level, has to be gone.

Blocking is computed on the grid, not on the artwork. A few pixels of offset between levels suggest partial overlap, yet one level 1 tile covers exactly one cell on level 0. In practice the ends of every row stay open, and the middle of that 12-column band only opens after the layer above it clears.

Circles only come in a single pair

This is the detail that changes how you read the board. The core set holds 34 faces and the board needs 56 pairs, so the deal is uneven. The first 22 faces in the list appear four times each. The last 12 appear twice.

In plain terms: the four winds, three dragons, all nine characters and bamboos 1 through 6 give you two pairs apiece. Bamboos 7, 8, 9 and all nine circles give you one. Once a pair of 7 circles leaves, that face is gone from the table for good.

That changes when a pair is worth spending. A free wind can wait, since two identical tiles are still hiding somewhere. A free circle cannot.

What each button costs

  • Hint (−3s): highlights a playable pair, no usage limit. Three seconds is about six clicks.
  • Shuffle (−5s): re-deals the faces of the remaining tiles and leaves each one standing where it was. It opens no new cell.
  • New game: reshuffles all 112 tiles and resets every counter. Costs no seconds. Costs the round.

The first one carries a trap. With no playable pair on the board, Hint still charges its 3 seconds before telling you there is nothing to show. When that message appears, the next click has to be Shuffle. Pressing Hint again just burns another three.

The arithmetic behind the mode

Bonuses do not stack above 5:00. Early on, every correct pair pushes the clock back to the ceiling and the surplus evaporates. Fast play at the start builds no reserve, it only avoids loss.

  • 56 correct pairs are worth 112 seconds of bonus.
  • One hint per pair would cost 168 seconds, 40% of everything the game hands out.
  • Three shuffles cost 15 seconds, more than the bonus from seven pairs.
  • A wrong pair costs 1 second and removes nothing. The tile you just clicked stays selected, so the click is not thrown away.

A clearing order that holds up

  • Top first. The four tiles on level 3 free the matching cells on level 2, and you need no reading at all to take them.
  • One row at a time, ends toward the middle. On a 12-tile row, pulling the end tile opens its neighbour, and the whole row becomes available without touching anything else.
  • Favor the pair that uncovers something. Between two playable pairs, the one sitting higher is worth more than the one at the edge, because the edge was already open.
  • Sweep in horizontal bands. Working one line down costs less than jumping between corner and middle on every move.

The final 30 seconds

At 30 seconds left the timer panel changes color. From that point the layer strategy stops paying: take the first playable pair your eye lands on and keep the hand moving. Each pair returns 2 seconds, so five quick ones buy ten seconds, and ten seconds is usually the gap between 54/56 and an empty board.

Three ways to burn the clock

  • Shuffling at the first block. If tiles remain on an upper level, the block clears itself in two moves. That is five seconds for nothing.
  • Hunting a flower. There is none in this deck. No wildcard is coming to finish an orphan pair.
  • Ignoring the pair counter. Reading 12/56 with the base untouched means almost everything you cleared came off the top. Time to drop a band.

When this mode is the wrong pick

If the clock spoils the fun, Relaxing Mahjong runs the same pairing mechanic with no deadline at all. Mahjong Pyramid shows a timer too, though it counts upward and only reports at the end. Mahjong Chain applies pressure differently, through 4.5 second combo windows.

Round one, five rules

Open Timed Mahjong when you are ready to play, take the four top tiles first, finish one row at a time, save Hint for a real block, and keep your hands off Shuffle until 30/56. The exception is a board that scatters the circles through the middle: spend a hint early there, because each circle is a one-off pair and gets harder to find once the table opens up.
