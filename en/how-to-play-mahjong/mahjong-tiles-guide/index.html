What Are the Mahjong Tiles: 144 in the Set and Only 34 Pictures

Count the tiles on a solitaire board and you get 112. Count the different pictures and you get 34. Everything else is a duplicate, and the duplicates are what make a pair possible.

112 tiles on the board, 34 pictures

Open Mahjong Solitaire and look at the whole board. There are 112 tiles, but only 34 distinct faces. Without copies there would be no pairs, and without pairs there would be no move.

People arriving from the browser game tend to assume hundreds of faces exist. There are 34 in the main deck and 42 in the full set. What changes from one board to the next is the number of copies, never the variety.

The arithmetic that lands on 144

A traditional set holds 144 tiles, and the split is worth memorizing:

  • 108 suited tiles: three suits of nine numbers, four copies of each.
  • 28 honor tiles: four winds and three dragons, four copies of each.
  • 8 bonus tiles: four flowers and four seasons, one copy of each.

Drop the bonus tiles and 136 remain, which is the Japanese set. Add eight jokers and you reach 152, the American one. The face count stays put across all three: 34 without bonus tiles, 42 with them.

Suit first, number second

Every suited tile answers two questions, always in this order. Which suit is it, and what number does it carry. No two suits write the number the same way, so recognition starts with the overall shape and only then moves to detail.

Beginners reverse it. They hunt for the number and give up before they get to the suit.

Characters: a numeral on top, a word underneath

The character suit is the friendliest one for anyone who does not read Chinese. The upper half carries the numeral, the lower half repeats the character that means ten thousand. Nine tiles in four copies, 36 in total.

The 1 Character and the 9 Character share that lower half, so the whole difference sits in the top stroke. On a crowded board, check the upper half before you commit to a pair.

Bamboo: the only tile with a bird on it

The 1 Bamboo shows no sticks at all. It shows a bird, and it is the only tile in the 144 with an animal drawn on it. From 2 to 9 the suit turns into a bundle of stalks you count by eye.

Another 36 tiles. Players who mix up bamboo and circles almost always stumble on the 1, which does not look like it belongs to the group.

Circles: dots you can count

Circles are discs stacked in columns. The 1 is a single large disc, and from 2 to 9 the dots group up so the count is literal. This is the easiest suit to check from across the table.

Thirty-six more, and the three suits close at 108.

Seven honors with no number at all

Honors belong to no suit. Four winds follow compass order, East through North, and three dragons separate by color: red, green and white. Four copies each, 28 tiles.

The white dragon is the odd one out. On a physical set it arrives blank, with nothing drawn on it, the only one of the 34 faces with no artwork. Here the suit name prints under the tile, which settles the question in a second.

Flowers and seasons match inside their own group

The eight bonus tiles never join a sequence or a triplet. Four flowers (plum, orchid, bamboo flower, chrysanthemum) and four seasons (spring, summer, autumn, winter) come numbered 1 to 4 and sit apart from the rest.

The rule almost nobody explains: any flower matches any flower, and any season matches any season. They are not four faces. They are two groups. That is how eight tiles produce four pairs without a single identical couple.

Two groups, eight tiles, and a single mode here puts them on the table: Classic Mahjong, with the full 144 in play.

Color carries the fast read

On the tiles at this site every suit has a fixed color: wind in dark blue, dragon and character in red, bamboo in green, circle in near black, flower in pink and season in violet. That is not decoration. With 112 tiles on screen the glyph shrinks, and color does work the eye has no time for.

One pair of suits color cannot separate. Dragon and character share the red, so the drawing takes over: a dragon is one large character, a character tile is a numeral plus a smaller word.

No mode here deals all 144

The counts move a lot. Solitaire builds 112 tiles, Relaxing 108, Tower 120, Blitz 76, Pyramid 60 and Memory 36. Connect and Chain run a thin deck of 16 faces, each repeated five times.

The full set shows up in exactly one place, Classic, with four players and the flowers in play. The comparison of the modes lays out which rule each table swaps, which helps before you click.

The twelve rare faces in Solitaire

The Solitaire deck holds 56 pairs across 34 faces, and 56 does not divide by 34. The first 22 faces come out four times. The last 12 come out twice.

In deck order those twelve are the 7, 8 and 9 Bamboo plus all nine circles. The practical result: a circle pair is unique on the board. If the partner will not show up, it is buried, not tucked into the far corner.

Five minutes to train the eye

Do not memorize 34 faces. Learn five groups and one order: suit, then number. On a real set, sort three piles by suit and a fourth for honors. Five minutes later you can name 108 tiles without reading a thing.

The cheapest practice is playing without a clock. Open Solitaire, ignore the timer, and say each tile's name out loud before you click. A wrong name costs nothing there. Turn order, draws and discards are covered in the how to play mahjong guide.

The exception is anyone heading for a four-player table. Then the eight bonus tiles join the count, and the group rule has to be clear before the first draw, because a flower never substitutes for a suited tile in any hand.
