How to Play Mahjong Connect: The Complete Path-Matching Guide

Mahjong Connect is the mode where you link identical tiles on a flat grid: the challenge is not the stack, it is the path. Learn the two-turn rule, the route tips and how to clear the board more often.

What is Mahjong Connect

Mahjong Connect (also known as "Shisen-Sho" in its classic form) is a one-player Mahjong mode whose goal is to clear the whole board by linking identical tiles. Unlike Mahjong Solitaire, the tiles here do not stack in layers: they sit on a flat grid, and the challenge is to find a free path between two matching symbols.

It is one of the most addictive modes on our site because it is easy to learn and hard to master. You can play free Mahjong Connect any time, right in your browser, with nothing to download. How the connection rule works

The core rule of Connect is different from the "free tile" rule of Solitaire. Here, two tiles with the same symbol can be removed if a path links them within a limit of turns.

The line with at most two turns

The path between the two tiles can be:

  • A straight line (horizontal or vertical) with no tile in between.
  • One turn (an L-shape): the line leaves one tile, bends 90° at an empty point and reaches the other.
  • Two turns (a Z or U shape): the line makes two detours but still passes only through empty cells.
Rule in short: two identical tiles can be joined if there is a route of at most three segments (two turns) that links them through empty space only. If the path would need three turns or more, the pair is blocked.

Tiles in the same row or column, with nothing between them, are always valid moves — and they are usually the easiest to spot when the board is full.

What counts as a valid pair

Only the symbol matters: the same flower, character or number. There is no suit or colour distinction like in the four-player classic game. That is why Connect trains your eye to recognise patterns fast — and why it works so well on small screens like a phone.

How to play Mahjong Connect step by step

  1. Scan the whole grid. Before clicking, find pairs in the same row or column — those are the "freebies" at the start.
  2. Click the first tile. It gets highlighted and the game shows the possible paths.
  3. Click the matching tile. If the route has up to two turns and is clear, both tiles vanish.
  4. Repeat until empty. The goal is to remove every tile before time runs out (in timer mode) or simply to win.
  5. Use Shuffle when stuck. No pairs available? The Shuffle button re-stacks the remaining tiles to open new moves.
  6. Use Hint if you need it. It highlights a valid pair so you are never trapped.

Tips and strategies to clear the board

Clear the edges and corners first

Tiles at the edges of the grid have more "space" around them, so it is easier to draw a path to them. Clearing the borders first usually unlocks the centre of the board.

Watch the central blocks

  • Don't leave isolated empty cells. A "hole" walled in by tiles can become unreachable within two turns.
  • Remove pairs that free the middle. Think two moves ahead: which removal opens the most new paths?
  • When four identical tiles are visible, take the most trapped ones. Keep the easy pair for the end, when the board is tight.
  • Use Hint and Shuffle with a clear head. They rescue a stuck board, but lean on them too much and you stop seeing the pairs yourself.

Mahjong Connect vs Solitaire: which is which

In Mahjong Solitaire, you clear pairs of "free" tiles from a layered pyramid. In Connect, the tiles sit on a flat grid and what matters is the path between them, not their position in a stack. Connect plays more like a maze game; Solitaire plays like a layered puzzle. Both use the same 144 Chinese tiles — only the mechanic changes.

Your next steps

To sum up: identical tiles join by a path of at most two turns, the board is flat and the goal is to empty it. Start slow, clear the edges first and use Shuffle when stuck. And of course, open a round of free Mahjong Connect and put this guide into practice right now.
