How to Play Classic Mahjong: Rules, Tiles, and How to Win

Classic Mahjong is the original four-player game, played with 144 traditional Chinese tiles. Learn the table rules, how to build the wall, and how to declare a win.

What Is Classic Mahjong

Classic Mahjong is the original four-player game, born in China in the late 19th century. Unlike the solo modes you find online, classic Mahjong is a social table game: four players use 144 tiles and race to build a winning "hand" before their opponents. What Tiles Are in Classic Mahjong

The traditional set has 144 tiles split into two big groups: the numbered suits and the honors. Each tile carries a carved symbol; matching identical tiles or forming runs is the heart of the strategy.

The three numbered suits

  • Dots (Balls): 1–9, drawn as discs.
  • Bamboo (Sticks): 1–9, shown as bamboo sticks.
  • Characters (Wan): 1–9, with the "ten thousand" ideogram and a number.

Each number of each suit appears in four copies, which gives 108 tiles from the suits alone.

The honors: dragons and winds

The honors never form runs — only groups of identical tiles. There are three dragons (Red, Green, White) and four winds (East, South, West, North), each in four copies — plus 8 optional flowers and seasons used as bonuses. In total, 144 tiles.

How to Set Up the Game

At the start, the tiles are shuffled and arranged into four walls (one per player), forming a square in the centre of the table. Each player is dealt 13 tiles; the player on turn (East, to begin) draws the 14th. From there, a round follows a simple rhythm: draw a tile, decide if it helps, then discard another.

The Goal: How to Win at Classic Mahjong

Winning means declaring "Mahjong" — that is, completing a 14-tile hand arranged into valid combinations. A winning hand usually has:

  • Four sets (each a pung of three identical tiles or a chow of three in sequence) and
  • one pair (two identical tiles) as the "eye".

Whoever builds that structure first calls the win. Rare combinations score bonus points, but the base is always the same.

Basic Rules of a Turn

  1. Draw. The player on turn takes the top tile from the wall.
  2. Discard. If the tile does not help, they return it face up to the table.
  3. React. Any opponent may claim, pung, or chi that discard to advance their own hand — if it makes sense.
  4. Pass the turn. Play moves clockwise until someone declares Mahjong.
Summary: draw, discard, someone may claim the discard, and the round continues. Whoever closes the hand of 4 sets + 1 pair wins.

Tips for Getting Started

Classic Mahjong looks intimidating on paper, but three habits solve most of the game in practice:

  • Go for runs first. Characters, bamboo, and dots form tracks from 1 to 9; holding neighbouring tiles is usually more flexible than chasing pungs.
  • Discard the least useful. Isolated tiles (a lone 1 of bamboo with no neighbours, a wind nobody drew) are the first to go back on the table.
  • Watch opponents' discards. What a rival returns tells you what they are not building — and what they may be hunting.
  • Do not ignore honors. Dragons and winds never form runs, so they only count as pungs; use them early or shed them fast.

With the 4 sets + 1 pair base in mind, every round becomes a tile-adding puzzle — and the fastest way to learn is to play. Open Classic Mahjong for free and follow the rhythm of the first few hands.

Classic vs. Solitaire and Connect: What's the Difference

The solo modes are puzzles; classic Mahjong is a table game with opponents. In Mahjong Solitaire you clear pairs from a pyramid alone; in Mahjong Connect you join identical tiles on a flat grid. In classic, four people compete and the luck of the discard matters as much as strategy.
